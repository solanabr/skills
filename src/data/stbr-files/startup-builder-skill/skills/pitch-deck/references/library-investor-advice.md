# What investors prescribe for a pitch

Sixty prescriptions from people who invest or run programs (YC, Paul Graham, NFX, First Round,
a16z, a16z crypto, a16z speedrun, Hustle Fund, Techstars, Colosseum and others), each as a short
quote with its source. Use it to see who says what, where they agree and where they contradict
each other, before you treat any one rule as settled. None of it is measured; for what has been
measured, see [library-investor-studies.md](library-investor-studies.md).

"Reported" means an editor or the firm's own summary relays the advice rather than the person
writing it. Quotes from videos come from auto-captions and keep their errors; "..." marks a cut.

## Consensus and conflict, by topic

- **One-liner:** say what the company does in one plain sentence, early (E01, E03). An analogy
  works if it is fresh, never a worn "Uber for X" (E02).
- **Cold email and intros:** everyone prefers a warm intro. Conflict on cold email: get introduced
  (E04) versus cold-email in parallel (E05). Format: about three sentences, a couple of KPIs, ask
  for the meeting (E06). Deck before the meeting: do not attach or send it (E07, E08) versus send a
  pre-read (E10, which promotes NFX's own tool).
- **First meeting:** pitch 8 to 10 minutes and talk the rest (E09); leave with an attempted close
  or a clear next step (E11); the CEO alone at the first meeting (E12).
- **Spoken pitch:** conflict on opening with the problem: start with the problem in hardware
  (E13) versus get straight to the point (E01) versus describe the change in the world (E14).
  Conflict on numbers: one in every sentence (E15) versus four or five at most (E16).
- **Q&A and objections:** do not bluff (E17). Conflict on raising risks yourself: yes, in the first
  ten minutes (E18, E20) versus no risk slide (E19).
- **Demo day:** one presenter (E21) versus a minute from a technical "expert witness" (E43); no
  video (E22); most of the prep goes to the first 30 seconds (E23); at Colosseum the pitch video
  is the first thing judges review and can decide the shortlist (E24).
- **Demo:** show it (E25) versus demos seldom work at demo day (E26). Colosseum changed from one
  video with the demo (E28, 2024) to a pitch without demo plus a separate technical video (E27,
  E29, 2025).
- **Deck content:** team at the end (E30) versus at the start (E41); competition throughout (E31)
  versus one slide near the end (E32); bottom-up market size (E33) versus no market size at all
  (E34).
- **Traction without revenue:** consensus that validation stands in for revenue: a specific LOI
  (E35), validation over small traction (E36), technical progress plus capital efficiency plus
  validated demand (E37), informal user feedback (E39). Raising on the vision and launching later
  is acceptable (E38).
- **Team:** sell why you are the team (E40); a credential counts only if it is domain expertise,
  which matters in complex or regulated spaces (E42).
- **Process and momentum:** in parallel (E52) versus slow and relationship-led, five firms at a
  time (E53). Avoid investors until you raise (E54) versus keep three to five warm (E55). The first
  term sheet speeds up the rest (E56).
- **Follow-up:** thank and close the open points (E58); update at every milestone (E59); weekly
  update videos at Colosseum (E60).

**Deep tech:** small rounds, each tied to a milestone (E57); an LOI or pre-sale in place of
revenue (E35); a non-expert version of the story (E44); shift between vision and detail (E45); a
technical FAQ before the partner meeting (E46); pull the investor into the vision for a moonshot
(E47); domain expertise in house (E42). The only hardware-specific source found (E13, E20) is
weak: self-promotional, no named author.

**Crypto:** explain new terms with concepts the audience already uses (E48); a different deck for
generalist and crypto-native investors (E49); no token bolted on for upside (E50); equity with
token rights rather than an early token commitment (E51). Colosseum's format: E24, E27-E29, E39,
E60.

**Not covered by any source found:** Lux, DCVC, Eclipse, Founders Fund and Shield publish theses,
not pitch tactics. Multicoin, Variant and Paradigm publish no pitch guide of their own. Nobody
addresses the "why blockchain?" question as pitch advice, or onchain metrics as traction.
